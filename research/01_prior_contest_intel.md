## 1. Ranked findings (most → least load-bearing)

### Finding 1 — The exact scoring rubric is known and almost certainly reused (VERIFIED)
HackerRank's own blog publishes the four-signal weighting from May, and the June `problem_statement.md` lists the **same three deliverables** (`code.zip`, `output.csv`, `chat_transcript`) + AI Judge interview, so the weights almost certainly carry over:

| Signal | Weight | What it grades |
|---|---|---|
| **Output CSV** | **30%** | Per-row accuracy vs golden labels + a "safety score." Empty/generic justifications **cap at ~70** even when the label is right. |
| **Code ZIP** | **30%** | Agent architecture 30% · prompt/tool craft 30% · robustness 25% · eng rigor 15%. |
| **AI Judge Interview** | **30%** | Technical depth/ownership 40% · problem judgment 25% · communication 20% · honesty 15%. |
| **Chat Transcript (AI Fluency)** | **10%** | Evidence *you* steered the AI, not vice-versa. |

Sources: [HackerRank blog — Behind the Scenes](https://www.hackerrank.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-hackerrank-orchestrate/), [official evaluation_criteria.md](https://github.com/interviewstreet/hackerrank-orchestrate-may26/blob/main/evalutation_criteria.md).

**Implication for June:** CSV is only 30%. You cannot win on CSV alone. The other 70% is *how you built it* and *how you defend it*. A mediocre VLM pipeline that you can explain brilliantly beats a great pipeline you can't defend.

### Finding 2 — Winners are "balanced builders," not specialists (VERIFIED, quantified)
From 1,349 scored submissions: **no two metrics correlated above 0.45**; interview-vs-code correlation was only **0.283**. **Every Top-10 was top-quartile on all four signals.** Sorting by any single metric would have found only **12 of the real Top-50**. Among Top-50, 37 were top-quartile on *all four*.
Source: [What 12,885 Developers Taught Us](https://www.hackerrank.com/blog/what-12885-developers-taught-us-about-building-with-ai/).
**Takeaway: budget time so all four artifacts are strong. Do not over-invest in CSV at the expense of the eval report, the README, or interview prep.**

### Finding 3 — The actual #1 winner's repo and architecture (VERIFIED — highest-value artifact)
**Saai Syvendra** (University of Moratuwa, Sri Lanka), age 20, won out of 12,885 registrants. His repo is public:
- Repo: https://github.com/saai-syvendra/hackerrank-orchestrate
- Writeup: https://saai.syvendra.com/myspace/hackerrank-orchestrate

His winning design (the patterns to copy):
- **"One agent, one tool."** He explicitly *rejected* multi-agent. A single agent with one `support_kb` tool exposing three modes — `search` (hybrid retrieval), `read` (full doc, the only thing that earns a citation), `grep` (exact token). "Reach for a multi-agent system only when a dire need forces you to."
- **Pre-LLM short-circuit:** cheap rules + Haiku 4.5 dispatch trivial/injection/off-topic tickets so the expensive model never runs ("Don't pay a genius to say you're welcome").
- **Hybrid retrieval:** BM25 + dense (OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large`) fused via **Reciprocal Rank Fusion**, then **Cohere `rerank-v3.5`**; LanceDB local store; **deterministic chunking** so the same input → same output every run.
- **Citation guard (post-hoc):** a citation only counts if the agent actually `read` that file; unverifiable replies are **auto-downgraded to `escalated`**.
- **"When in doubt, escalate":** a confident wrong answer is treated as worse than an honest handoff.
- Stack: Claude Sonnet 4.6 (agent) + Haiku 4.5 (classifier), LangChain `create_agent`, **Anthropic prompt caching on the system prompt**, a real `eval_sample.py` harness, optional Textual TUI.

This corroborates HackerRank's own finding: **single-agent + tools/RAG had the best rank (#1), beating graph workflows (best #2) and multi-agent pipelines (best #3).**

### Finding 6 — The AI Judge interview, characterized (VERIFIED)
30 min, **camera mandatory**, conversational not adversarial (the judge says things like "don't worry, take your time"). It has access to your code and probes, in order: opening pitch/problem framing → failure modes & architecture → **code-level technical deep-dive** → production viability & monitoring → **how you directed your AI tools**.
Grading: **technical depth/ownership 40%, problem judgment 25%, communication 20%, honesty 15%.**
The decisive behavioral signal HackerRank reported:
- **Winners used ownership language:** *"I chose X because…", "I rejected Y because…"*, and explained *when* they added a component after seeing a *specific* failure.
- **Losers narrated the AI:** *"Claude created the pipeline", "the agent decided the answer."*
Sources: [Behind the Scenes](https://www.hackerrank.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-hackerrank-orchestrate/), [winner writeup](https://saai.syvendra.com/myspace/hackerrank-orchestrate), [What 12,885 Developers Taught Us](https://www.hackerrank.com/blog/what-12885-developers-taught-us-about-building-with-ai/).

### Finding 7 — The chat transcript (AI Fluency, 10%) is graded on *steering* (VERIFIED)
Graders read `~/hackerrank_orchestrate/log.txt` for **clear, scoped prompts and evidence you critiqued, verified, and drove the AI**. "You — not the AI — should be visibly steering the architectural decisions." Cheap to win: a clean transcript showing planning, rejected approaches, and verification beats a messy "make it work" log.

### Finding 8 — Cautionary public data points (VERIFIED)
- The Medium writeup author ([Akshay Kulkarni](https://medium.com/@akshayjk0701/building-an-ai-support-triage-agent-in-24-hours-my-hackerrank-orchestrate-2026-experience-b635f436b459)) chose pure **TF-IDF + rule-based** and — importantly — **did not complete the AI Judge interview**, forfeiting 30%. Lesson: finishing all four artifacts dominates polishing one.
- Many forks (e.g. `nameershah`, 3★) went **stdlib-only / BM25-only / no LLM in the answer path** for determinism. Defensible, but plain lexical retrieval underperforms on paraphrase and multilingual inputs — and the winner *did* use LLMs + reranking, so "deterministic-only" is not the ceiling.

## 2. What top submissions likely did → what to copy for the multi-modal task

**Verified transfers (proven winning patterns):**
1. **Single agent + a small toolset, not a multi-agent zoo.** `[INFERENCE]` For June: one agent with tools like `inspect_image(image_id)`, `get_user_history(user_id)`, `get_evidence_requirement(claim_object, issue_type)`. Resist a 5-agent pipeline.
2. **Force structured output + validate it.** Use tool-calling / structured output bound to the **14-column schema with strict enums**, then **Pydantic-validate every field against the allowed-value lists** (`claim_status`, `issue_type`, per-object `object_part`, `risk_flags`, `severity`) with one retry → safe fallback. The enum surface in June is *large* — invalid enums are free points lost. (Schema confirmed in [problem_statement.md](https://github.com/interviewstreet/hackerrank-orchestrate-june26/blob/main/problem_statement.md).)
3. **A deterministic guard layer that maps to the rubric's "safety score."** The May winner auto-downgraded unverifiable replies to `escalated`. `[INFERENCE]` June analog: if no image actually supports the claim, force `claim_status=not_enough_information` and `supporting_image_ids=none`; if image quality is bad, set `valid_image=false` + the matching `risk_flag` (`blurry_image`, `low_light_or_glare`, `cropped_or_obstructed`, `wrong_angle`); cap severity at `unknown` when undeterminable.
4. **Cheap-model short-circuit.** `[INFERENCE]` Use a cheap pass to reject obviously invalid/wrong-object images before spending a full VLM call — directly satisfies June's **required operational analysis** (model calls, tokens, cost, TPM/RPM, batching/caching).
5. **Ground every justification in evidence and cite image IDs.** The rubric **caps generic justifications at ~70%**. The June schema literally says justifications should "mention relevant image IDs." Every `claim_status_justification` must reference the specific `supporting_image_ids` and what's visible.
6. **Determinism:** temperature 0, fixed image-read order, cache VLM results per image hash. Reproducibility is explicitly graded.
7. **Ship a real `evaluation/` folder.** June *requires* it: metrics on the 20 labeled `sample_claims.csv`, **≥2 strategies/prompts/models compared**, the chosen strategy, and an operational analysis (`evaluation_report.md`). The winner had `eval_sample.py` with per-field normalized matching and citation-consistency checks — replicate this for the 14 columns (per-column accuracy + a confusion matrix on `claim_status`).
8. **Use the multi-modal signals June hands you that May never had:**
   - `evidence_requirements.csv` is a **rubric for `evidence_standard_met`** — map each `(claim_object, issue_type)` to its `minimum_image_evidence` rule and check it explicitly.
   - `user_history.csv` (`history_flags`, `rejected_claim`, `last_90_days_claim_count`) drives the `user_history_risk` flag and tempers justifications — *the spec explicitly says use history for risk context, not to overturn image evidence.* Don't let history flip a clearly-supported image.
   - The `user_claim` field is a **multilingual** chat transcript (sample row 2 is Hindi/Romanized) → multilingual handling is back; the **prompt-injection vector is now `text_instruction_present`** (text embedded *in an image* telling the reviewer what to conclude → flag, don't obey).

**Interview prep to copy (40% of 30%):** Walk in able to say *"I chose a single VLM agent because…", "I rejected per-object sub-agents because…", "I added the evidence-requirement check after seeing the sample mislabel case_00X…".* Name a concrete failure mode and your mitigation. Be explicit about what you designed vs. what Claude Code generated.

## 3. What to avoid (each tied to a verified signal)

- **Don't over-engineer a multi-agent system.** Multi-agent's *best* rank was #3; single-agent won. Complexity you can't defend in the interview hurts you twice.
- **Don't skip or thin out the interview/eval artifacts.** They're 70% combined; the Medium author forfeited 30% by not interviewing.
- **Don't ship generic justifications.** They cap at ~70% even when the label is correct. Every justification must cite the image and the visible evidence.
- **Don't narrate the AI in the interview or transcript.** "Claude built the pipeline" is the documented loser pattern. Use "I chose / I rejected / I verified."
- **Don't emit invalid enums or wrong column order.** The June schema is strict (14 columns, exact order, per-object `object_part` lists). Validate programmatically — this is the cheapest 30% to protect.
- **Don't hardcode test-specific answers** (June explicitly forbids "hardcoded test labels or file-specific answers" — graders read the code).
- **Don't ignore cost/ops.** June *requires* an operational analysis; a solution that fires an uncached VLM call per image with no batching/throttling story loses robustness/eng points.
- **Don't let `user_history` override clear image evidence.** History is risk *context* only; flipping a supported image to contradicted on history alone is a likely failure-mode trap.
- **Don't assume "deterministic-only / no LLM" is safe.** It's defensible but the winner used VLM+rerank+guards; pure lexical/rule pipelines underperform on paraphrase and multilingual claims.
